# 2/1 Game Force Continuations (H-22 Drill)

Convention: After a 1-suit opening, a new suit at the 2-level by an unpassed
responder (13+ HCP) is **game-forcing**. Neither player may pass below game.

## Quick Reference

| Opener Rebid | Meaning |
|--------------|---------|
| 2M (own major) | 6+ cards, any strength |
| New suit | 4+ cards, natural |
| 2NT | 12-14 balanced |
| Raise responder | 4+ support |
| 3M (own major) | 6+ cards, extras |
| 3NT | 15-17 balanced |

Responder with 3-card support for opener's major: bid 2/1 first, then
raise to 3M later (forcing -- opener chooses 4M or 3NT).

---

## Hand 1 -- Basic 2/1: 1H-2D, Opener Rebids 2H

  South (opener): ♠ K 7 3   ♡ A Q 10 8 5  ♢ 4 2     ♧ K J 6
  North (you):    ♠ A 8 4   ♡ 7 6         ♢ A K J 7 3 ♧ Q 9 2

  North: 14 HCP, 5 diamonds, only 2 hearts. No 4-card major fit.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1H    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Your response?

> 2D (2/1 GF -- 13+ HCP, 5+ diamonds)

  South rebids: 2H (6+ hearts, minimum)

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1H    │ Pass │ 2D    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2H    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Your rebid?

> 3NT

  3NT -- correct! You have 14 HCP, stoppers everywhere (♠A, ♢AKJ, ♧Q),
  no heart fit (only 2). Opener showed 6+ hearts and minimum -- 3NT is
  the right game. Don't bid 4H with only 2 trumps.

  Key lesson: After opener shows 6+ in a GF, responder places the game.
  With 2 trumps and stoppers, 3NT is right.

## Hand 2 -- Delayed 3-Card Support: 1S-2C, Raise to 3S

  South (opener): ♠ A J 9 7 5  ♡ K 3       ♢ A 10 4   ♧ 8 5 2
  North (you):    ♠ K 8 6      ♡ A J 4 2    ♢ 7 3      ♧ A Q 7 4

  North: 15 HCP, 3 spades, 4 hearts, 4 clubs. No 4-card spade fit for
  Jacoby 2NT, but you have 3 spades and game-force values.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Your response?

> 2C (2/1 GF -- 13+ HCP, 4+ clubs)

  South rebids: 2S (6+ spades, minimum)

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2C    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2S    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Your rebid?

> 3S (delayed 3-card support, forcing)

  3S -- correct! This is THE critical 2/1 bid. You show 3-card support
  in a GF auction. Opener knows: you have exactly 3 spades + 13+ HCP.
  With 6+ spades (AJ975), opener bids 4S -- 8-card fit guaranteed.
  With only 5, opener would choose between 4S and 3NT.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2C    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2S    │ Pass │ 3S    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 4S    │ Pass │ Pass  │ Pass │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Final: 4S. The 3-card support route finds the major-suit game that a
  simple 2S raise (6-9 only) would have missed.

  Key lesson: With exactly 3-card major support and 13+ HCP, bid 2/1
  first, then raise to 3M. This is the hand type that H-22 was built for.

## Hand 3 -- After Opener's 2NT: Responder Places Contract

  South (opener): ♠ K Q 8 5 3  ♡ A 4       ♢ K J 7    ♧ Q 10 3
  North (you):    ♠ J 7 2      ♡ K Q 8 5    ♢ A 10 6 2 ♧ A 4

  North: 15 HCP, 3 spades, 4 hearts. You bid 2D (2/1 GF).
  Opener rebids 2NT (12-14 balanced, exactly 5 spades).

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2D    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2NT   │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Your rebid?

> 4S (3+ support for opener's known 5-card major)

  4S -- correct! Opener's 2NT shows 12-14 balanced with exactly 5 spades.
  You have 3 spades. That's an 8-card fit. With 15 HCP opposite 12-14,
  combined count is 27-29: game is clear, slam is unlikely.

  Wrong answer: 3NT. You have a known 8-card spade fit. The 5-3 major
  fit almost always plays better than NT.

  Key lesson: After opener's 2NT, a balanced rebid confirms exactly 5 in
  the opening suit. With 3+ support, bid 4M rather than passing 3NT.

## Hand 4 -- Fourth Suit is Natural in GF

  South (opener): ♠ A Q 9 5 3  ♡ K 4       ♢ J 7 2    ♧ A 8 5
  North (you):    ♠ 7 4        ♡ A 10 8 6 3 ♢ A K 5    ♧ K Q 3

  North: 16 HCP, 5 hearts. You bid 2H (2/1 GF). Opener rebids 2S (6+)...
  but opener actually rebids 3C (4+ clubs, natural shape-showing).

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2H    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 3C    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Opener showed spades + clubs. Three suits bid. Your rebid?

> 3D (natural, 4+ diamonds -- the fourth suit is NATURAL in GF)

  3D -- correct! In a 2/1 GF auction, fourth suit is natural. You are
  showing real diamonds (AK5), not asking for a stopper. This helps
  both sides evaluate for 3NT (stoppers) or 5D (fit).

  Wrong thinking: "3D = fourth suit forcing, asking opener for a diamond
  stopper." That's the non-GF treatment. In 2/1, every bid is already
  forcing, so fourth suit shows a real suit.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2H    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 3C    │ Pass │ 3D    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 3NT   │ Pass │ Pass  │ Pass │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Final: 3NT. Opener has Jxx in diamonds (enough with your AK5), no
  heart fit, and balanced enough for NT.

  Key lesson: Fourth suit = natural in 2/1 GF. No artificial "fourth
  suit forcing" needed when everything is already forcing.

## Hand 5 -- Competitive Interference: Pass is Forcing

  South (opener): ♠ A K J 8 5  ♡ 7 3       ♢ K Q 4    ♧ K 10 2
  North (you):    ♠ 9 6        ♡ A K J 4    ♢ A 8 7 3  ♧ Q 6 5

  North: 14 HCP. You bid 2D (2/1 GF). East overcalls 3C.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2D    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2S    │ 3C   │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Opponents interfered. Your bid?

> 3NT (placing the contract -- clubs stopped by Q65)

  3NT -- correct! The GF survives interference. You have a club stopper
  (Q65 behind the overcaller), no spade fit (only 2), and game values.
  Place the contract directly.

  Also acceptable: Pass (forcing). This says "I have nothing useful to
  say about clubs, but we're still game-forcing -- you must bid." Opener
  would then bid 3D, 3H, or 3NT. But here, with a club stopper and
  clear direction, bidding 3NT is better than passing the decision.

  Wrong answer: Pass intending to let them play 3C. NEVER. The 2/1 GF
  is irrevocable -- pass is FORCING, not a sign-off.

  Key lesson: After interference in a 2/1 auction, pass = forcing (partner
  must act). Double = penalties. New suit = natural and forcing.

## Hand 6 -- Competitive Interference: Penalty Double

  South (opener): ♠ K Q 10 7 5  ♡ A J 3     ♢ A 5 2    ♧ 8 4
  North (you):    ♠ 8 3         ♡ K 7        ♢ K J 9 4  ♧ A Q J 10 3

  North: 14 HCP. You bid 2C (2/1 GF). West overcalls 3C (preemptive).

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2C    │ 3C   │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  West stole your suit! Your bid?

> Double (penalty -- AQJ103 in their suit, GF means we don't need takeout)

  Double -- correct! In a 2/1 GF auction, we're already committed to game.
  We don't need a takeout double. Double here says "I think we can beat
  3C." With AQJ103 sitting over the 3C bidder, this is a juicy penalty.
  Partner can pull with extreme shape, but should usually pass.

  Expected result: 3CX down 2-3 = +300 to +500. Better than 3NT which
  requires 9 tricks.

  Key lesson: In a GF auction, double is penalty-oriented. You're already
  committed to game -- use double to punish, not to ask for takeout.

## Hand 7 -- Cuebid Slam Exploration

  South (opener): ♠ A K J 9 5  ♡ 4 3       ♢ A K 8    ♧ Q 7 2
  North (you):    ♠ Q 10 7 3   ♡ A K 8 2    ♢ 6 5      ♧ A K 3

  North: 17 HCP, 4 spades. Normally Jacoby 2NT with 4-card support, but
  suppose the auction went: 1S - 2H; 3S (6+ spades, extras). Spades agreed.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2H    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 3S    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Spades agreed, opener showed 6+ with extras. Your bid?

> 4C (cuebid -- club control, slam interest)

  4C -- correct! With 17 HCP opposite a hand that showed extras (3S = 6+,
  good hand), slam is possible. Cuebid your cheapest first-round control.
  4C shows the ♧A or void in clubs. You bypass diamonds (4D) -- this
  DENIES diamond control (first-round).

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2H    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 3S    │ Pass │ 4C    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 4D    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Opener cuebids 4D (diamond control). Your next bid?

> 4NT (RKCB -- both side suits controlled, check keycards)

  4NT -- correct! Opener showed diamond control (you denied it by
  bypassing). Now all side suits are controlled (♧ by you, ♢ by opener,
  ♡ by your AK). Ask for keycards.

  Opener: 5C (30-41-22: 3 or 0 keycards). With AK of spades + A of diamonds
  = 3 keycards. You have Q of trumps + A of hearts = 2 keycards. Total:
  5 keycards + trump queen = 6S.

  Key lesson: Cuebid up the line after fit is agreed. Bypass = denial.
  Once controls are confirmed, use RKCB 4NT.

## Hand 8 -- 5m vs 3NT: The Stopper Test

  South (opener): ♠ 7 3       ♡ K 10 4     ♢ A Q J 8 5 ♧ A K 3
  North (you):    ♠ Q 8 4     ♡ A 7 6 2     ♢ K 10 7 3  ♧ J 5

  South opens 1D. You bid 2D? No -- 1D-2D is a raise, not 2/1. Instead:

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1D    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  13 HCP, 4 diamonds, 4 hearts. Your response?

> 1H (not 2/1 -- bid 4-card major at 1-level first)

  Correct! Always show a 4-card major at the 1-level before considering
  2/1. Opener rebids 2D (6+ diamonds). You raise to 3D (invitational)...
  but wait, you have 13 HCP. Bid 3NT instead? Let's think.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1D    │ Pass │ 1H    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2D    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Diamonds agreed (6+ from opener + your 4). 3NT or 5D?

> 3NT (stoppers in all side suits: ♠Q84, ♡A762, ♧J5 is thin but opener has ♧AK)

  3NT -- correct! When in doubt, bid 3NT. You have 9 tricks available:
  5+ diamonds + side winners. The spade stopper (Q84) is thin but real.
  5D would need 11 tricks -- much harder.

  Wrong: 5D. Only bid 5m when you have NO stopper in a side suit. Here
  spades is covered (Qxx) and clubs by opener's likely holding.

  Key lesson: 'When in doubt, bid 3NT.' 9 tricks beats 11 tricks. Only
  escape to 5m with a clear stopper problem.

## Hand 9 -- 1S-2H: The Special Sequence

  South (opener): ♠ A Q 10 7 4  ♡ K 3       ♢ Q 8 5    ♧ K J 2
  North (you):    ♠ 8 5         ♡ A Q J 9 7 2 ♢ A K 3   ♧ 10 4

  North: 14 HCP, 6 hearts. After 1S, you bid 2H (2/1 GF, 5+ hearts).

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2H    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2NT   │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Opener rebids 2NT (12-14 balanced, 5 spades). Your rebid?

> 4H (6+ hearts, self-sufficient suit, plays better than NT)

  4H -- correct! You have AQJ972 -- a self-sufficient 6-card heart suit.
  Even opposite K3, that's an 8-card fit (or 7 with AQJ972 playing well).
  4H is safer than 3NT with only ♧104 as a club stopper.

  Wrong: 3NT. Your clubs are wide open (104). If they lead clubs, you
  need to run tricks fast. 4H with a 6-card suit is much safer.

  Key lesson: After opener's 2NT, bid 4M with a long suit that plays
  better than NT. Self-sufficiency matters more than stopper count.

## Hand 10 -- The Catch-All 2C with No Good Suit

  South (opener): ♠ K J 9 7 5  ♡ A 8       ♢ K Q 4    ♧ 10 7 3
  North (you):    ♠ Q 4 2      ♡ K J 5 3    ♢ A 7 6    ♧ A Q 8

  North: 16 HCP, 3 spades, 4 hearts, 3 diamonds, 3 clubs. No 5-card suit,
  no 4-card spade fit for Jacoby 2NT. What do you bid?

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ ?     │      │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Your response?

> 2C (catch-all 2/1 GF -- 4 clubs acceptable when no better suit)

  2C -- correct! With 16 HCP and no 5-card suit, 2C is the "catch-all"
  GF bid. It creates the force while leaving maximum room. You'll show
  3-card spade support later with 3S.

  ┌───────┬──────┬───────┬──────┐
  │ South │ East │ North │ West │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 1S    │ Pass │ 2C    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 2S    │ Pass │ 3S    │ Pass │
  ├───────┼──────┼───────┼──────┤
  │ 4S    │ Pass │ Pass  │ Pass │
  └───────┴──────┴───────┴──────┘
  Final: 4S via the delayed 3-card support route. Opener had 5 spades +
  your 3 = 8-card fit. Game bid.

  Key lesson: 2C is the most flexible 2/1 bid. Use it as a catch-all
  when no 5-card suit is available but you have 13+ HCP.
