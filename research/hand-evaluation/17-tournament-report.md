# 1. The winner

**Two-lens** (Zar when the partnership has a major fit, Fifths when it plays notrump) won the
round robin **15–0**. **All Fives** (our system after DEC-15: Zar with a fit, HCP in notrump)
was second at 14–1, losing only to Two-lens, by 0.02 IMP per board. **SAYC** (HCP with the
classic 25 / 33 / 37 lines) finished 13th.

**Format.** 16 teams, one per evaluation method. Every pair played one 10 000-board match on
plain random deals (no constructed hands). Each board was played at both tables with the
same cards, scored double-dummy in IMPs, then converted to victory points (WBF 20-VP scale).
Dealer rotates N/E/S/W and vulnerability follows the standard 16-board cycle. Every team uses
its own vulnerable and non-vulnerable lines; the dealer has no effect, because this model has
no competitive auction.

# 4. What the analytics say

- **Most boards are partscore or game decisions.** Of 70 000 random boards, 50% are
  partscore deals, 41% game deals, 7% small-slam deals and 2% grand-slam deals. Yet the
  slam and grand deals account for about a third of every team's losses (section D):
  slam judgment is worth far more than its frequency suggests.
- **Why the winner wins.** Two-lens is right 71% of the time overall. Compared with plain
  HCP, it finds more games (68 missed per 1 000 boards instead of 92) and more slams (33% of
  slam deals instead of 21%), with slightly fewer failed games (140 per 1 000 boards instead of 144).
- **Why SAYC finishes 13th.** It is the most cautious team: it plays partscore on 65% of
  boards. It is the best of all on partscore deals (89% right) but misses **187 games per
  1 000 boards** and loses 1.49 IMP per board on game deals alone. **Caveat:** double-dummy
  play is clairvoyant (every finesse works when it should), so it rewards aggressive game
  bidding more than real play does. Some of SAYC's deficit would shrink at a real table.
- **Two-lens vs All Fives** differ only on notrump boards (losses 1.77 vs 1.82 IMPs per
  board there). On major-fit boards they are identical because both use Zar.
- **One count does not fit all.** Zar alone is best-equal on major-fit boards (−2.90) but
  among the worst on notrump boards (−2.37). Fifths is the reverse (−3.20 / −1.77). The two
  composites take the best of each.
- **The slam zone separates the methods most.** At 32-34 combined HCP the losses range from
  5.6 (BUM-RAP) to 11.4 IMPs per board (classic LTC). Shape-based counts (NLTC, HCP + length
  + shortness, classic LTC) collapse there because they overrate distribution when the
  partnership is already strong.
- **Grand slams are hard for everyone.** Even the winner gets only 16% of grand deals right.
  Zar with its published 67 line bids the most grands (39% right) but fails about 14 per
  1 000 boards.
- **Vulnerability raises the stakes.** Every team loses about 30% more per board when
  vulnerable, because game and slam swings are bigger.
- **The featured match shows the shape of the win.** Two-lens beat SAYC by 0.74 IMP per
  board. SAYC won many small swings (2 796 boards of 3-5 IMPs, mostly partscores where it
  stopped low), while Two-lens won the big ones (6 985 boards of 10-13 IMPs, mostly games
  and slams).

# 5. Method and limits

- **Data:** 100 000 uniformly random deals (seed 1000), double-dummy solved in all strains.
  Thresholds were tuned on deals 1–30 000; the tournament used the untouched deals
  30 001–100 000. The 10 000-board matches rotate through seven blocks of those deals.
- **Teams:** each team differs only in its **hand evaluation**. Both partners' counts are
  pooled (perfect exchange). The strain is the same for all (an 8-card major fit, otherwise
  notrump), and the partnership with more HCP declares. The choice is partscore, game,
  small slam or grand.
- **Not modelled:** opponents' bidding, competitive auctions, sacrifices and doubles,
  opening leads, single-dummy play, keycard checks, minor-suit games, and strain choice.
  These are simulations, not table results.
- **OHE (Darricades):** a codification from Neil Timm's overview, not the book. The book's
  published lines are on a different scale from this codification, so the "OHE full @pub"
  result says nothing about Darricades himself (`14-ohe-codification.md`).

  - `eval_lab tournament data/battle-100k-s1000.jsonl 0.3 10000`
  - `eval_lab analytics data/battle-100k-s1000.jsonl 0.3 10000 out.md`
